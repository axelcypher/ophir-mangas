# The catalogue is series, editions and volumes; the edition is what a grid shows

Ophir Figures keeps one flat row per product. Manga are collected volume by volume within one publisher's edition, and the same series exists in German, Japanese and English editions with different numbering, titles and prices. So the shared catalogue has three levels: a series (the work), its editions (publisher, language, format, numbering, list price) and their volumes (number, ISBN, release date, cover). The catalogue and the collection show editions as tiles, each opening to its volumes; a switch shows single volumes or groups by series, publisher, author, genre or language. Decided with the user on 2026-11-07.

## Considered options

- Series and volumes only, with publisher and language on each volume: German and Japanese volumes of a series would mix in one numbering.
- Flat volumes as in Figures: no notion of a complete edition, so no gaps.
- Series as the tile: progress is per edition, one level further down.

## Consequences

- A Limited Edition is a variant: a volume row of its own (own ISBN, own picture) with the same number as the standard volume. Owning either counts for that number; gaps and progress count numbers, not rows.
- A volume without a number (fanbook) is a special volume and never a gap.
- Reading status belongs to the series (the story), the read mark to the volume, and Collecting to the edition: one account may read in German and collect the Japanese edition.
